I am an Australian macroeconomist building a public, reproducible R pipeline that extracts forward estimates and historical actuals from every Australian state and territory Budget Paper, Mid-Year Economic and Fiscal Outlook, and Final Budget Outcome, and renders them as an interactive Quarto dashboard deployed to GitHub Pages. The dashboard should let a user explore state fiscal positions individually and in aggregate, across years and across forward-estimate vintages, with drill-down on the variables that matter for macro analysis: revenue by source, expenses by economic type and by function, net operating balance, capital expenditure, net debt, and public sector wage policy decisions.
This is a sibling project to my existing repo at https://github.com/DavidAStephan/NOM_Nowcast and should follow the same engineering conventions: renv for package management with a committed renv.lock, targets for orchestration, DuckDB for the data warehouse, Quarto with GitHub Actions for deployment, testthat for tests, tidyverse style guide enforced by styler and lintr. R-only, CRAN-only, no Python dependencies. Treat the project as an installable R package with DESCRIPTION, NAMESPACE, and functions organised under R/. Before writing any code, please first read the existing NOM_Nowcast repo to absorb its conventions around vintage tracking, the structure of _targets.R, the use of config.yml via the config package, the layout of R/ingest, R/models, R/backtest, and the way Quarto pages are organised and built in CI.
The site must contain seven page types. First, a landing page summarising the project and current state of fiscal positions. Second, eight state-by-state pages (NSW, VIC, QLD, WA, SA, TAS, ACT, NT) showing historical actuals and the most recent forward estimates for revenue, expenses, capex, and net debt, with drill-downs by category. Third, a cross-state comparison page with interactive controls. Fourth, an aggregate-states view summing across jurisdictions. Fifth, a forward-estimate vintage page showing how estimates for a given variable and a given out-year have evolved across budget updates. Sixth, a public sector wage policy tracker. Seventh, a methodology page documenting sources, extraction approach, harmonisation choices, and known limitations. All pages must be interactive, with client-side filtering and plotting using plotly and crosstalk and reactable, because the site is fully static and deployed via GitHub Pages with no server. Do not use Shiny.
Coverage spans FY2014-15 onwards, eight jurisdictions, three document types per jurisdiction per year (Budget Papers around May or June, Mid-Year updates around November or December, Final Budget Outcomes around September following the financial year, with WA Budget in May and NSW Budget release timing varying). That's roughly 265 documents in total, with forward estimates within each Budget Paper typically covering the budget year plus three out-years.
For each document, extract the General Government Sector forward estimates table. The variable set covers revenue (total revenue, taxation revenue including payroll tax, stamp duties, land tax, gambling taxes, motor vehicle taxes, other taxes; GST revenue; Commonwealth grants tied and untied; sales of goods and services; interest income; dividend and tax-equivalent income; other revenue), expenses by economic type (employee expenses, superannuation, depreciation, interest, other operating, current grants and subsidies, capital grants), expenses by function aligned to GFS COFOG (health, education, public order and safety, transport and communications, social security and welfare, general public services, recreation culture and religion, other), balance sheet and capex variables (net operating balance, net capital investment, net lending/borrowing, cash surplus/deficit, net debt, net financial liabilities, net worth, purchases of non-financial assets, total infrastructure investment program where reported), public sector wage policy details (policy text, annual rise percent, effective dates, coverage, productivity offsets, sign-on bonuses), and the economic parameters each Budget contains (GSP growth, employment growth, unemployment rate, CPI, WPI, population growth).
Primary sources are public PDFs and Excel files on each state Treasury or Budget website: budget.nsw.gov.au, dtf.vic.gov.au/state-budget, budget.qld.gov.au, ourstatebudget.wa.gov.au, statebudget.sa.gov.au, treasury.tas.gov.au, treasury.act.gov.au, and budget.nt.gov.au. URLs change over time, so maintain a registry of current document locations rather than hardcoding URLs. Cross-validate against ABS Government Finance Statistics annual (cat. 5512.0) and quarterly (cat. 5519.0.55.001), pulled via readabs. Population for normalisation comes from ABS cat. 3101.0 and GSP from cat. 5220.0, also via readabs. Commonwealth Grants Commission reports are useful for GST distribution cross-checks.
The DuckDB warehouse uses a star-ish schema. A single fact table fiscal_facts holds every fiscal observation keyed by jurisdiction, variable_id, fiscal_year, value in AUD millions, with flags for whether it's a forward estimate, the estimate type (budget, MYEFO, outcome, GFS), the source document_id, extraction timestamp, extraction method (rule_based, llm_assisted, manual_override), and free-text notes. Dimension tables cover variables (with category, sub-category, unit, is_flow flag, and GFS code), jurisdictions (with full name, FY end date, budget release month), and documents (with type, fiscal year, release date, source URL, PDF path, parser version, ingestion timestamp). A separate wage_policy table holds the extracted wage policy statements with parsed annual rise percentages, effective dates, coverage descriptions, and notes. Every estimate is tagged with the document it came from and that document's release date, so the system can reconstruct "what was knowable about FY2024-25 NSW net debt as at any past date" by filtering on dim_document.release_date — this is the same vintage discipline the NOM_Nowcast project uses, and is what powers the revision history view.
PDF extraction is the hard part. The approach is per-jurisdiction parser modules under R/extract/, one per state, versioned, with parser version recorded in dim_document. Rule-based first using tabulapdf to extract the General Government Sector forward estimates tables, which appear in predictable locations (typically BP1 Appendix A or similar) and have consistent structure year-over-year within a state. Where rule-based parsing fails — typically older years or format changes — fall back to LLM-assisted extraction using the ellmer package with the Anthropic API at build time only. Critically, the production pipeline that runs in CI must never call an LLM: the LLM-assisted extraction step runs once on a developer machine, the structured output is committed to data/extracted/ in the repo, and the production warehouse build reads from those committed CSVs. This keeps production purely R plus structured public data, which matters for institutional deployment. Manual overrides for edge cases live in data/manual_overrides/overrides.csv and take precedence in the warehouse build. Validate every extracted figure against ABS GFS where available; discrepancies above 2% raise a flag in STATUS.md.
Harmonisation is the genuinely hard analytical part and is the real intellectual contribution of the project. State chart of accounts differ; the same economic concept may be called different things in different jurisdictions. Build a variable_dictionary.csv in inst/ mapping canonical_variable to jurisdiction to source_line_item with mapping_notes, maintained manually and version-controlled. For revenue, expense, and capex, lean on ABS GFS categories as the canonical reference since GFS flows into national accounts and is what the RBA ultimately cares about. The methodology page must document every non-trivial harmonisation decision.
The repo structure is: _targets.R and quarto.yml at root, config.yml for parameters via the config package, DESCRIPTION and NAMESPACE for the package, renv.lock and .Rprofile for environment management, a .github/workflows/render.yml that runs the pipeline and deploys to GitHub Pages, R/ containing ingest/ (budget_docs_registry.R, download_pdfs.R, abs_gfs.R, abs_population.R, abs_state_accounts.R), extract/ (one extract<state>.R per jurisdiction plus extract_wage_policy.R), harmonise/ (variable_dictionary.R, chart_of_accounts.R, reconcile_gfs.R), warehouse/ (schema.R, write_facts.R, vintage_store.R), analytics/ (aggregates.R, per_capita.R, per_gsp.R, revision_history.R, wage_policy_panel.R), viz/ (theme_fiscal.R, timeseries_plots.R, comparison_plots.R, revision_plots.R), and utils/. data/ holds raw/ (downloaded PDFs, gitignored), extracted/ (structured CSV from LLM-assisted extraction, committed), manual_overrides/ (committed), and warehouse.duckdb (gitignored). inst/document_registry.csv holds all known budget documents with URLs, release dates, and parser versions. reports/ holds index.qmd, state_pages/nsw.qmd through nt.qmd, comparison.qmd, aggregate.qmd, revisions.qmd, wage_policy.qmd, and methodology.qmd. tests/testthat/ holds unit tests for every non-trivial function. README.md and STATUS.md at root.
